# 07-09-26:22:13

**Focus:** Diagnosed why the fixed sampler still produced structureless samples, traced it to the noise schedule leaking global structure at 1216², and replaced it with a resolution-shifted cosine.

## Theory

- **The samples were the prior mean, not the climatology.** Measured on the run's `samples.pkl`: mean over water 0.52, spatial std 0.074, 0.00002% of water pixels below 0.05 — against 0.271 / ~0.40 / 62% in the data. 0.52 in data range is exactly 0.0 in model range. The model was emitting a constant zero field plus a coastal gradient that is a one-layer function of the mask channel.
- **The linear schedule does not destroy large-scale structure at this resolution.** Noise is i.i.d. per pixel, so pooling over k×k cuts noise std by k while a smooth `x0` keeps its magnitude: `SNR(k) = sqrt(ᾱ/(1−ᾱ))·k`. At t=999 that is 0.006 per pixel but **7.73 at k=1216**. Confirmed empirically (96 fields × 5 seeds): `corr(pool(x_T), pool(x_0))` = +0.26 at k=64, +0.51 at k=152, **+0.73 ± 0.03 at k=1216**. So `q(x_T)` is nowhere near `N(0,I)`, the network reads the global layout off its input instead of learning to generate one, and a true `N(0,I)` draw at sampling time pools to ≈0 → the measured 0.52.
- **The leak is a function of image size** (0.41 at 64², 7.73 at 1216²), which is why an absolute β range does not transfer. Shifting log-SNR by `2·log(ref/d)` cancels exactly that growth: domain SNR 7.73 → 0.0032, measured corr → +0.001.
- **The ε-loss got *less* informative after the change, not more.** The shortcut `ε̂ = x_t` has MSE ≈ `ᾱ_t·E[x0²]`, and the shifted schedule has lower ᾱ at every t — so bucket losses drop across the board for reasons unrelated to sample quality. This contradicts what the plan predicted. Analytic only; the empirical confirmation was started but not run.
- **The x̂₀ clamp from last session is what lets ε-prediction survive the shift.** `ᾱ_T` falls to 6.7e-12, but the quantity that matters is `posterior_mean_coef_x0 × 1/sqrt(ᾱ)`, which stays ≤0.04 at every t except t=999 (31.6) — and there the clamp bounds the contribution to `coef_x0 × 1 = 8e-5`. Without it this schedule would have required v-prediction.

## Design decisions

- **`shift_ref_resolution=64`, not 128 or 32.** Endpoint leakage is closed by any of them; what separates them is how many steps actually synthesise coarse structure (64px-scale SNR < 1): linear 94, ref=128 **95**, ref=64 **186**, ref=32 344. ref=128 buys the endpoint fix and nothing else, so it is the conservative fallback only.
- **Plain cosine rejected.** It closes the endpoint leak but holds ᾱ high then crashes, leaving **11/1000** coarse-structure steps — worse than the linear schedule it replaces. The shift, not the cosine, is the operative part.
- **v-prediction deliberately deferred**, on the user's one-thing-at-a-time scoping and because the clamp analysis above shows ε-prediction is not actually broken here. Revisit if the shifted run still hedges toward the mean.
- **Geometric augmentation rejected as a data-expansion route.** Flips and rotations teach invariances the target distribution does not have — the task is to learn one specific geography, and sampling uses the true land mask, so mirrored training data is off-distribution. Real daily data is the answer instead.
- **Resolution and conditioning left alone** (user's call): full 1216², unconditional. That is what made the schedule fix mandatory rather than optional.

## Code design

- Schedule now defined on **ᾱ**, with β recovered as `1 − ᾱ_t/ᾱ_{t-1}` clamped to ≤0.999 — because every claim that matters is a statement about ᾱ, not β. `build_schedule()` in [models/ddpm.py](../../models/ddpm.py) replaces `beta_schedule`/`get_alphas` but **keeps the same attribute names**, so `q_sample`, `p_sample` and `sample` are untouched.
- **Invariant:** ᾱ is recomputed by `cumprod` from the clamped βs rather than reused from the analytic form. Skipping this leaves ᾱ and β disagreeing and silently breaks the posterior identities.
- **float64 must extend through the posterior coefficients**, not just the schedule. They all divide by `1 − ᾱ`; in float32 that cancellation is what produced `coef_x0[0] = 0.999834`. It is now exactly 1.0. Getting this wrong was caught only because the invariant check asserted it.
- **The land mask is resolved before the schedule** and `image_size` is derived from it — the mask is the authority on field size, and the shift depends on it. Passing a wrong `image_size` would silently mis-shift.
- **β is non-monotone under this schedule and that is correct** — U-shaped, ~0.030 at t=25, minimum 0.0062 at t=494, clamp at t=T. A constant log-SNR shift leaves `dλ/dt` alone but moves the ᾱ=0.5 point much earlier. Monotone β is a linear/plain-cosine convention, not a requirement; `check_invariants` asserts it for `"linear"` only.

## Open threads

- **The leakage test is easy to make lie.** An early version used 24 fields sampled every 12th index — all January — and reported +0.16 for linear and −0.36 for shifted, i.e. the opposite of the truth. It needs both n ≳ 96 (correlation SE ~1/√n, worst at the largest scale) and fields spanning the seasonal cycle, or there is no between-field variance to detect.
- **Never sampled at 1216² with the new schedule.** The smoke test was the 128² TEST region, where the shift is mild, and it mutated `ddpm.timesteps` to 50 after building a 1000-step schedule — that exercised the code path for NaN/crash only, it is not a valid short sampler.
- **Undertraining is untouched** — still 288 optimizer steps. Nothing about sample quality should be expected from this change alone.
- **Data expansion confirmed feasible but not started:** CDS `reanalysis-pan-carra-means` accepts `time_aggregation: "daily"` and covers **1985–2025**, versus the current monthly/2000–2023 — 8,766 fields instead of 288 for daily 2000–2023 alone. Monthly means also blur the marginal ice zone, so daily data would sharpen the ice edge the model is failing to produce.
